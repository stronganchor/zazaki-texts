Tale of the Miller and the Fox

There was once a miller. His mill was working. One day he stopped the millstone, went home, slept at home that night, got up in the morning, came to the mill, and saw that there was no flour in the bin.

The next night he kept watch and saw that at midnight a fox came in, went to the bin, and took out the flour. The miller got up, took a club, and struck the fox. When he caught it, the fox cried out.

The fox said to the miller, "Let me go. I will ask for the daughter of the Pasha of Egypt for you." The miller said, "I am a miller; how will you ask for the daughter of the Pasha of Egypt for me?"

The fox said, "Do not kill me. I will ask for her for you; if I do not ask for the daughter of the Pasha of Egypt for you, then kill me. Let me go."

The miller said, "Swear it to me." The fox swore to the miller, and the miller let the fox go. The fox set out, went to Egypt, went to the Pasha of Egypt, and paid his respects.

The Pasha of Egypt said to the fox, "What do you want? Tell me." The fox said, "My lord, allow me to speak with you."

The Pasha of Egypt gave the fox permission. The fox said, "My lord, Çemçequ Pasha has come; he is asking for your daughter for himself." Lerch marks the following bracketed words in the source as unclear and leaves them untranslated.

The Pasha of Egypt said, "Go to meet him with riders, come with him, and bring Çemçequ Pasha to me. There is a vizier there; I do not know the name of any Çemçequ Pasha." The fox said, "My lord, give me a set of clothing.

I will go to Çemçequ Pasha and he will put it on. For two days do not order your army to go out to meet him; until Çemçequ Pasha comes here, I will inform you." The Pasha of Egypt gave clothing to the fox.

The fox took the clothes, went off, and went to the miller. The fox said to the miller, "I have asked for the daughter of the Pasha of Egypt for you. Get up, go to the bath, wash and clean yourself, come, put on these clothes, and we will go to the Pasha of Egypt."

The miller got up, went to the bath, washed himself, shaved his head and beard, came, and put on the clothes. The fox led him away, and they went to Egypt. There they stopped.

The fox went and informed the Pasha of Egypt, saying, "Çemçequ Pasha has come. Give me a fine carriage; I will bring Çemçequ Pasha, and let the army come out to greet him." The Pasha of Egypt gave the fox a carriage, and three kawasses went ahead of the fox.

Çemçequ Pasha arrived in Egypt, and the army of the Pasha of Egypt gave him honor. The miller did not know how to return the greeting. Some of the soldiers said, "He is drunk, he is drunk."

Others said, "He is not a pasha." Others said, "He is a miller." The fox went back and forth and said to the soldiers, "It is hot; Çemçequ Pasha has fainted.

There is no sense in his head." The fox brought Çemçequ Pasha to the palace of the Pasha of Egypt. He got out of the carriage, and the fox took him by the hand, led him into the palace and the serail, and said to the Pasha of Egypt, "For a month there has been no sense in this head."

The Pasha of Egypt said, "Lead him to the bedroom and put him on the bed so that he may rest." The fox led Çemçequ Pasha to the room and put him on the bed. Çemçequ Pasha trembled.

The fox said, "You cursed man, do not be afraid. Go lie down." The miller said to the fox, "The Pasha of Egypt will come now and cut off my head."

The fox said, "Do not be afraid. I will not leave you. Keep your senses.

If the Pasha of Egypt comes into the room now, get up and kiss his feet." Çemçequ Pasha said to the fox, "Now the Pasha of Egypt will come and kill me." The fox said, "Get up.

I will prepare water for you in the basin. Go, perform ablution, come back in, and pray." The miller said to the fox, "Bring me water; I will go and wash."
